# Editorial Guidelines

## Purpose

This document defines the editorial standards of The Coherence Project.

Its purpose is to ensure consistency across every publication, regardless of format, language or publication date.

---

# Editorial Philosophy

The objective of every publication is to increase understanding.

Writing is not intended to impress the reader.

Writing is intended to help the reader think more clearly.

---

# Voice

The project's voice should be:

- Clear
- Thoughtful
- Curious
- Honest
- Respectful
- Calm
- Evidence-oriented

The writing should communicate confidence without arrogance.

---

# Tone

The tone should remain professional while being approachable.

Complex ideas should feel accessible.

The project avoids unnecessary formality without becoming informal.

---

# Writing Principles

Every publication should strive to:

- Explain before concluding.
- Define concepts before using them.
- Prefer precision over persuasion.
- Prefer clarity over sophistication.
- Prefer depth over novelty.
- Respect the reader's intelligence.
- Respect the reader's time.

---

# Structure

Whenever appropriate, long-form publications should follow a logical progression:

1. Context
2. Problem
3. Observation
4. Reasoning
5. Evidence
6. Implications
7. Conclusion

Not every publication requires every section, but the reasoning should always be explicit.

---

# Examples

Examples should clarify ideas rather than entertain.

Whenever possible, examples should come from real organizations, practical experience or observable situations.

Hypothetical examples should be clearly identified.

---

# Terminology

Concepts should always use their official terminology.

Definitions should remain consistent across every publication.

When a new concept is introduced, it should be documented in the project's terminology guide.

---

# Citations

Whenever external ideas influence the work, sources should be acknowledged.

The project values intellectual honesty over perceived originality.

---

# Revisions

Publishing is part of the research process.

Documents may evolve as understanding improves.

Meaningful revisions should be versioned.

---

# Quality Checklist

Before publication, ask:

- Is the main idea clear?
- Is every important concept defined?
- Is the reasoning easy to follow?
- Does every paragraph contribute?
- Could any sentence be simpler?
- Would this still be valuable in five years?

If not, continue refining.
